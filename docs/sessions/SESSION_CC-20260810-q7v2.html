What changed

Replaced the previous single-file guide, which used an Insurance worked example, with a new build on a Restaurant example: Project Mise en Place at Harbor & Vine Hospitality Group — 42 casual-dining restaurants, $128.4M revenue, $40.3M of food purchases, introducing AI-assisted demand forecasting and prep planning. The company is fictional and is labelled as such in three places in the guide.

The page is structured as a help-centre knowledge base rather than a document: a left topic nav with active-section tracking, a global search box with / focus and arrow-key result navigation, and an Ask the Guide assistant that answers from 36 Q&A entries embedded in the page. The assistant makes no network calls and works with the machine offline; off-topic questions fall through to ranked search suggestions rather than inventing an answer.

Export mechanism

Download HTML produces a self-contained deliverable per document: Colaberry cover with the embedded logo, document-control strip, berry-blue tables, callouts, a Prepared / Reviewed / Approved sign-off block and a footer, with the shared SVG marker definitions injected so diagrams render standalone. Save as PDF opens that identical designed document in a print dialogue, so the PDF matches the HTML. Download CSV serialises the document’s tagged tables with a UTF-8 byte-order mark so Excel opens them cleanly.

Quality bar — enforced, not asserted

The MoSCoW correction

The first pass of the requirement set came out at 63% Must, which is the exact defect the guide teaches architects to catch. Rather than leave it, FR-03, FR-12, NFR-A2 and NFR-P2 were demoted to Should and FR-17 and NFR-P3 to Could, landing at 29 Must / 19 Should / 4 Could / 9 Won’t across 61 requirements — 48% Must, under the 50% ceiling the guide sets.

The correction is propagated to the donut chart, the story backlog, and the change log in D8, and is written up in-guide as a worked teaching example rather than hidden.

  • Zero orphan requirements; the RTM records the one that was found and removed at baseline, with the reasoning
  • Every non-functional requirement carries a number, a measurement point and a condition
  • Every user story carries at least one negative acceptance criterion
  • Financials internally consistent end to end: $535K one-time, $200K per annum run, $1.412M annual benefit, $1.81M NPV at 12%, 11-month payback, 34% break-even realisation factor
  • The thin Could bucket (4 items) is called out in the guide rather than passed over
